What is a Mac notch app?

A Mac notch app is a utility that draws content into and around the camera cutout at the top of a MacBook screen — the strip of display macOS otherwise leaves black. Instead of wasting that space, a notch app uses it for things you would otherwise go looking for: the track that's playing, files you're moving between apps, the clock, the battery, your next meeting.

The idea is borrowed from the Dynamic Island on the iPhone, which turns its own cutout into a live status area. macOS ships nothing equivalent, so the MacBook notch stays empty by default. FunNotch is a free one for macOS 14 and later, on both Apple Silicon and Intel.

Focus sessions that actually block things

Start a timer and the sites and apps you named stop being available until it runs out. The countdown stays in the closed notch, so you can see how long is left without opening anything.

  • Blocks whole apps, not just their websites
  • Pomodoro cycles with breaks, and a running session total
  • Pauses your music, and can fire a Shortcut at each end
  • Hides the game while you're focusing, which is the point

Also in the box

Everything else this Mac notch app does

None of this has an animation above, and all of it ships today.

It replaces the system HUD

Volume, brightness and keyboard backlight appear in the notch — and the big grey square in the middle of your screen doesn't appear at all. It catches the key before macOS acts on it, so there's never one of each.

Your menu bar, your call

Pick the icon or have none at all, and put a live readout next to it: what's playing, battery, the focus countdown, your next event, or how many things are on the shelf.

AirDrop off the shelf

Send what's sitting there to another Mac or an iPhone without opening Finder. The full macOS share sheet is one click further, so Messages, Mail and everything else is there too.

Music from a browser tab

YouTube, Spotify web, SoundCloud, Apple Music, Bandcamp, Twitch. Not the tab title — the page itself, so you get the real title, artist, artwork and a progress bar that moves.

Scrub, shuffle, repeat

Drag the progress bar to seek. Shuffle and repeat are right there, showing the player's actual state rather than a guess at it.

Invisible to screen recording

Optionally excluded from captures and screen shares, so a demo of your app doesn't come with a notch full of your calendar.

Sneak peeks

The track changes and the closed notch briefly says what it is, then gets out of the way. Same for volume, charging and connecting headphones.

Colour taken from the artwork

The panel tint, the progress bar and the spectrum can all pull their colour from whatever is playing. Or ignore it and use your accent colour, or one you pick.

Calendar & reminders

This week at a glance, today's agenda, and a Join button that finds the link inside Zoom, Meet, Teams and Webex invitations.

Battery & power

Charge level in the header, and a live activity when the charger goes in or comes out.

Bluetooth activities

Devices announce themselves as they connect and disconnect, with battery level where the device reports one.

Downloads & screenshots

Finished downloads and new screenshots are announced and can go straight to the shelf. Browser placeholder files are ignored until they're real.

Mirror

A camera preview in the corner of the panel, round or square, for checking your hair before the call.

Themes

Six presets, plus save your current look under your own name. Accent, tint, border, progress and spectrum colours.

Gestures and haptics

Two-finger swipe down to open, up to close, with adjustable sensitivity — and a trackpad tap when it opens. Or just hover. Or click.

Any display

Notch or no notch, one screen or five. Set the size, border width and corner radius to match your Mac, and let it follow your pointer between displays or stay put.

Stays out of the way

It steps aside for fullscreen video, and backs off its polling when the screen is locked or asleep, so an idle Mac stays idle.

Diagnostics

One screen saying exactly what the app can and can't currently see, and what to do about each gap. Permissions that were never granted stop looking like bugs.

First launch: this build is signed ad-hoc rather than with a paid Apple Developer certificate, so macOS refuses it the first time and says it's damaged or from an unidentified developer. It's neither — that's what Gatekeeper says about anything it can't check with Apple. Drag it to Applications, open it, let it be refused, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the bottom and click Open Anyway. On macOS 14 you can instead right-click FunNotch in Applications and choose Open — that shortcut was removed in macOS 15. The full install guide covers both, what the warning actually means, and every permission the app asks for afterwards.
