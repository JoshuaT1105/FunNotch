The two best-known free Mac notch apps, side by side. FunNotch started as a recreation of TheBoringNotch — a genuinely good, open-source app that got there first — and has since grown past it. This is the short list: the things you'd actually notice about what each puts in your MacBook notch, including the ones where theirs still wins.

Which should you use?

If you want the most in the notch today — clipboard history, focus blocking, a HUD that actually replaces the system one — use this.

If you want software you can read and audit, that a community stands behind, and that will still be maintained in a year, use theirs. It's open source, it has a team, and it got here first. No feature table beats that.

Who each one actually suits

Feature tables are bad at this, because they imply the app with more ticks wins. Both fill the MacBook notch with roughly the same kind of thing — a Dynamic Island for a Mac, more or less — so the choice depends far more on which of two risks bothers you more.

Pick TheBoringNotch if the project matters as much as the app. Both are now GPL-3.0, so you can read every line of either — but theirs has contributors, releases and issue threads going back much further, which means if the original author stops, someone else can continue. It is also notarised by Apple, so it opens on first double-click with no warning and no detour through System Settings. That last one is a real, daily-life difference and this page is not going to pretend otherwise.

Pick FunNotch if you want the features now. Clipboard history, focus sessions that actually block sites, a HUD that replaces the system one instead of sitting next to it, and twelve configurable widgets are all here today. Several of them are on TheBoringNotch's published roadmap, and given how quickly they ship, "today" is the only honest claim this page can make.

The uncomfortable version: FunNotch is one person's spare-time project with no community around it. The source is on GitHub and GPL-3.0, so it could be picked up if I stopped — but nobody has a reason to yet, and "someone could fork it" is not the same as a team. That is a real difference and the table above cannot show it, which is why it is written here instead.

What switching costs

Very little, in either direction, which is the useful thing to know before spending an evening on this.

Both are ordinary Mac apps that keep their settings in their own preferences and their state on your machine. Neither has an account, so there is nothing to migrate and nothing to cancel. You can install one, use it for a week, and drag it to the Trash having lost nothing but the time.

Do not run both at once. Two notch apps drawing into the same strip of screen will fight over it, and both will look broken. Quit one before launching the other. If you want to compare properly, give each a few days on its own rather than switching back and forth in an afternoon.

The one thing that does not survive a switch is your permission grants. Accessibility, Calendar and Camera are granted per app, so whichever you move to will ask again. That is macOS being careful rather than either app being awkward.

The signing difference, in practice

This is the one row in the table that changes your first five minutes rather than your daily use, so it is worth spelling out.

TheBoringNotch is signed and notarised with a paid Apple Developer certificate. You download it, you open it, it opens.

FunNotch is signed ad-hoc, because a Developer ID costs $99 a year and this is a free app made by one person. macOS will refuse the first launch and tell you it cannot verify the developer. Getting past it takes about twenty seconds through System Settings, and there is a page here that walks through it — but it is a real inconvenience and it is fair to weigh it.

What that warning means is worth being precise about, because it is the same warning malware produces. It means Apple has not checked the app, not that Apple checked it and found something. If that distinction does not comfort you, that is a perfectly sound reason to use the notarised one — and what FunNotch does and does not send over the network is written down for anyone who wants to weigh it properly rather than guess.

How this page is kept honest

The TheBoringNotch column is taken from their own website and README, not from testing their app, and it was read on 1 August 2026. Not listed means the feature did not appear in either place on that date — it does not mean the app cannot do it, and it certainly does not mean it never will.

A comparison page written by one of the two apps is a biased source and you should treat this one accordingly. The mitigation here is that the rows where theirs wins are in the same table as the rows where this one does, at the bottom, not omitted. Go and read theirs before deciding.
